## 0. Constitutional Roadmap Anchor

**Reveal structure. Preserve judgment.**

Every future feature is subordinate to this rule. Future scale may add accounts, Supabase persistence, professional sharing, AI assist, transits, travel mode, mobile UX, better geocoding, style presets, certification, education, Web3-like ownership experiments, or version 2.0 workflows. None of those future systems may change the platform’s core moral contract: the software reveals geographical chart structure; the human user remains responsible for interpretation.

The roadmap exists precisely so tomorrow’s ideas do not contaminate today’s code. Future features should be named, preserved, and sequenced, but they must not be smuggled into active implementation as half-built assumptions. If a feature belongs to later product maturity, it stays in this roadmap until explicitly promoted by a focused architecture document, implementation plan, validation gate, and rollback path.

## 2. Roadmap Separation Doctrine

### 2.1 Future inventory is not active instruction

A roadmap item is not a command to implement. It is a preserved possibility, milestone, or planned direction. Before becoming active work, it must receive a current decision, narrowed scope, implementation plan, validation method, and rollback route.

### 2.2 Future concepts must not create hidden dependencies

Today’s backend should not assume Supabase tables that do not exist. Today’s map should not depend on Rain/Virga animation. Today’s professional workflow should not require AI interpretation. Today’s saved searches should not depend on Web3 object ownership. Future concepts must be designed so the active core remains usable without them.

### 2.3 Future AI must remain downstream

AI can become powerful, but it remains downstream of factual chart structure and user intent. Future AI can translate intent, suggest alternative searches, summarize comparisons, explain tradeoffs, generate reviewed exports, or help lay users learn. It cannot become the hidden authority that decides where the user should move.

### 2.4 Version 2.0 must preserve version 1’s truth contract

A future 2.0 product can be more conversational, persistent, mobile, AI-assisted, collaborative, and visually refined. It still must preserve popup truth, map-first exploration, human judgment, and separable fact/interpretation layers.

## 3. Product Roadmap: Chart-Centric Platform

### 3.1 Account and chart library

The platform’s commercial future depends on moving beyond a single local map file into chart-centric persistence. Users should be able to create, edit, delete, and select chart records. Professionals should manage multiple clients and charts. Each chart should own favorites, saved searches, comparisons, notes, and settings snapshots.

This is future-facing until the persistence layer is deliberately implemented. It must not be confused with temporary local JSON or ad hoc frontend state.

### 3.2 Dashboard and chart detail surfaces

Future product structure includes landing, account creation, dashboard, chart creation, chart detail, map exploration, favorites, comparisons, notes, shared views, settings, and diagnostics. The dashboard should orient the user without becoming map clutter. Chart detail is the object-management hub for one chart. The map remains the exploration instrument.

### 3.3 Saved searches and replay

Saved searches are central commercial objects. A saved search should preserve semantic conditions, chart context, settings snapshot, viewport, render metadata, and replay honesty. It should not preserve random renderer internals as product truth. Users and professionals need to name, reopen, compare, share, and export investigations.

### 3.4 Favorites and saved locations

Favorites should support named cities and arbitrary coordinates. They belong to a chart record. They can carry notes, be opened as relocated charts, be added to comparisons, and be included in exports. Rankings should be cautious or avoided by default because the product does not treat places as universally good or bad.

### 3.5 Comparison sets

Future comparison screens should show multiple candidate locations side by side, probably starting with two and carefully expanding to three or more where usable. Comparisons should show relocated facts: planet-in-house, angle signs, aspect-to-angle, relevant notes, and intentions. They should not automatically declare a winner unless the user explicitly asks for ranking under declared criteria.

### 3.6 Shared and client views

Professional sharing is a major future value. A professional may share a curated map with selected overlays, saved searches, notes, or candidate areas. Clients may be allowed to zoom, inspect, or perhaps mute/solo layers. They should not silently alter professional-selected conditions unless permissions explicitly allow it. Shared views should be elegant, minimal, and free of debug clutter.

## 4. Professional Ecosystem Roadmap

### 4.1 Professional workspace

A professional workspace should support multiple clients and charts under one account. It should preserve client-specific favorites, searches, comparisons, notes, and exports. This makes the product a working environment, not a one-off map toy.

### 4.2 Professional training

Future professional-facing training should explain the recommended workflow: select desired placements, search for overlaps, add NOT/exclusion conditions, inspect point truth, save candidates, compare charts, and use shared views. Training can include written guides and videos.

### 4.3 Certification and referral ecosystem

A later certification or referral system may help lay users find trained relocation astrologers. This is not active scope. It requires governance because certification creates authority and possible advice expectations.

### 4.4 Custom ontology dictionaries

Professionals may eventually define or choose ontology dictionaries: condition groups, dignity helper layers, preferred aspect families, interpretation vocabularies, or specialty frameworks. These dictionaries must not alter Layer 1 truth. They configure symbolic language and defaults.

### 4.5 Professional sovereignty

Every professional ecosystem feature must preserve astrologer control. AI may assist; it must not replace the professional’s symbolic reasoning or client relationship.

## 5. AI and Assistive Systems Roadmap

### 5.1 Consumer intake AI

Future consumer AI may ask what the user wants to move toward, what they want to move away from, known cities, constraints, birth-time certainty, and exploratory temperament. It can translate intent into proposed search conditions, but those conditions must be visible and editable.

### 5.2 Professional assist AI

Professional AI can help search alternatives. If the desired condition is unavailable in viable places, it can suggest adjacent symbolic strategies: aspect-to-angle alternatives, moving a difficult placement out of a house, or finding a stronger compensating condition. The professional remains in charge.

### 5.3 Comparison AI

Future comparison AI may summarize factual differences under user-stated intention. It should say what facts differ, what tradeoffs appear, and what alternatives may be worth inspecting. It must not present an unqualified winner as product truth.

### 5.4 AI interpretation boundaries

Future AI interpretation must label fact, interpretation, uncertainty, and user intent. It must preserve symbolic humility, avoid flattery, avoid fabricated biography, and reject oracle language. The user’s judgment remains final.

### 5.5 AI review and governance tooling

Future internal AI reviewers can audit Cursor or developer outputs for hallucination, doctrine drift, missing validation, rollback risk, or architecture overreach. Reviewers should be governed by prompt protocols and source-truth requirements.

### 5.6 AI model/version registry

Later maturity may require prompt-version, model-version, and output-provenance registries. This is future operational infrastructure, not active product code.

## 6. Travel, Transits, and Current Location Roadmap

### 6.1 Current location mode

Future product flows may include current location as a psychological and practical anchor. Users may compare natal location, current location, and candidate relocation locations. Current location should remain a coordinate fact, not a tracking obligation.

### 6.2 Transits to natal versus relocated houses

The roadmap preserves optional transit systems. The user has noted that transits to natal houses may feel more reliable personally, while some astrologers may want transits to relocated houses. Therefore any future relocated-house transit mode needs clear warnings, settings, and explanation. It must be optional, not silent default.

### 6.3 Travel mode

Travel mode is a later differentiator. It could notify users when planets change relocated houses, enter angle-aspect orb zones, or cross meaningful thresholds while traveling. This applies to flights, road trips, and extended movement.

### 6.4 Offline and airplane support

Future travel mode should consider downloading routes or coordinate sequences before departure. GPS may function without cellular data or Wi-Fi. The app could use precomputed chart/route data and local device location to show changes during travel.

### 6.5 Notification governance

Transit/travel notifications must preserve judgment. They can say “you are entering the selected zone” or “this condition is now closer to exactness.” They must not create fear-based alerts or deterministic claims. Notification settings must be deliberate and user-controlled.

## 7. Rendering and Experience Roadmap

### 7.1 Canonical renderer migration

Future architecture plans include moving production visible overlays from legacy GeoJSON to canonical screen-space adaptive truth after validation. This migration remains governed by adapter boundaries, smoke gates, no hidden auto-fallback, and rollback.

### 7.2 Aspect aura/material strips

Future aspect-to-angle rendering should refine material-strip/aura language around exact centerlines. The aura communicates intensity/proximity, not membership. Defaults may vary by aspect family and user settings. Popup and exact line remain authoritative.

### 7.3 Cusp-gradient display

A future cusp-gradient prototype can show house boundary softness, often discussed around a small default such as ~2°. It must remain distinct from aspect aura and should include explanatory affordances.

### 7.4 Overlap child colors

Future visual refinement should replace naive alpha mud with semantic child colors or adaptive overlap rendering. Overlaps are discovery objects and should remain readable under city labels.

### 7.5 NOT/exclusion visual refinement

NOT/exclusion layers need calm desaturating or redacted treatments. They should not become punitive red danger overlays. They should communicate intentional deprioritization.

### 7.6 Style presets

Future style presets may change palette, material finish, and emotional tone while preserving identical underlying truth geometry. Possible families include technical, organic, premium lifestyle, and gentle new-age-adjacent styles. Presets must not change math or certainty.

### 7.7 Rain and Virga reveal systems

Rain and Virga remain future temporal visual grammar. Rain reveals selected conditions. Virga ghosts sibling conditions and fades before completion. They must be driven by truth or clearly limited reveal pacing. They must not become fake solver animation.

### 7.8 Motion accessibility

Any future reveal or animation system must respect reduced-motion preferences, professional fast mode, and long-session comfort.

## 8. Mobile, Geocoder, and Cartography Roadmap

### 8.1 Mobile and tablet ergonomics

Mobile will require a compact drawer or bottom-sheet model. Full sidebar and Photoshop-style layer panels will fail. Mobile controls should prioritize quick focus, inspect, mute/solo, and search.

### 8.2 City and geocoder strategy

City search, label density, international spelling, transliteration, population ranking, relevance ranking, and disambiguation remain major future UX problems. Google Maps or another provider may offer better city density and labels but must be evaluated for cost, control, and product fit.

### 8.3 City labels under overlays

City labels are sacred under overlays. Future renderer and palette work must preserve city readability. Dense urban regions need special attention.

### 8.4 Arbitrary point inspection

Future mobile and desktop UX should preserve arbitrary point inspection. The product must not become city-only. Long-press or alternative mobile gestures may replace right-click on touch devices.

## 9. Infrastructure and Scalability Roadmap

### 9.1 Supabase migration

Supabase is a planned future persistence layer. It should support users, professional workspaces, clients, chart records, saved locations, saved searches, comparisons, notes, shared views, settings snapshots, and possibly AI output provenance. It requires row-level security, access policies, migration strategy, and replay honesty.

### 9.2 Accounts, authentication, and permissions

Account systems must support basic users, professionals, clients, shared viewers, and potentially team/workspace roles. Authentication and authorization must be designed before shared/client views become commercial.

### 9.3 Cache maturity

Future cache work includes production Phase-C integration, cache-key invariant enforcement, chart-change invalidation, substrate-flip separation, storm/drainage tests, and possible persistent caching only after privacy and invalidation contracts are mature.

### 9.4 Validation infrastructure

Future validation maturity includes golden screenshot CI, brute-force wall fixture registry, popup-overlay parity harness, edge-case suites, doctrine drift checks, and audit-generation tooling.

### 9.5 Export and PDF infrastructure

Future export may include client PDFs, shareable maps, saved-search reports, professional-branded cards, and comparison documents. Exports must declare whether they are exploratory views or authoritative factual records.
