# Visual Exploration Boards — Phase 2.9B

Status: exploration (conceptual concept-boards in prose)
Type: landscape of possibilities — described, not drawn
Constraint: no implementation, no production UI, no CSS, no component
construction, no mockups, no layouts, no implementation plans, no final design,
no doctrine synthesis, no winners chosen. Generate possibilities only.

Goal: not to find the answer but to *see the landscape*. Each concept is a
direction described in words. All concepts live *within* the established doctrines
(Plate, Metadata, Control, Table, Visual Hierarchy, Surface Treatment, Color,
Typography, Spatial, Family Resemblance) — used here as constraints, not rewritten
or challenged.

A reminder carried into every board: the wheel is the genetic center; identity
sits close and certain, interpretation drifts outward; nothing decides for the
user; the felt target is a contemplative instrument with an editorial hand.

---

## TASK 1 — Ten Profile page concepts

For each: wheel / plate / notes / metadata relationship + emotional character.

**1. The Observatory.** Wheel large and centered like a viewed sky; plate a quiet
caption beneath it; notes tucked at the periphery as a margin; metadata a faint
instrument-readout near the base. *Character:* reverent, studious, a little awed.

**2. The Hearth.** Wheel warm and central but not huge; plate intimate and
identity-forward beside it; notes a near, inviting presence; metadata folded into
the plate as personal facts. *Character:* homecoming, settled, personal.

**3. The Ledger.** Wheel present but the reference tables share strong billing;
plate compact and factual; notes as marginalia beside the tables; metadata
tabular and precise. *Character:* working, exact, accountant-of-the-self.

**4. The Atelier.** Wheel as the piece on the easel; plate a small signature;
notes a sketchbook always open nearby; metadata kept off to a tool tray.
*Character:* creative, exploratory, hands-on.

**5. The Reading Room.** Wheel composed at the top of a single reading column;
plate a titlepage above it; notes flowing beneath like commentary; metadata a
colophon at the foot. *Character:* literary, linear, contemplative.

**6. The Cockpit.** Wheel central with controls and readouts arrayed close around
it; plate a status line; notes a logbook to one side; metadata live gauges.
*Character:* instrument-like, alert, capable. (Risk: drifts toward control-clutter
if controls crowd the wheel — held in check by doctrine.)

**7. The Shrine.** Wheel dominant and glowing, given great stillness; plate a
minimal dedication; notes deliberately distant; metadata nearly hidden.
*Character:* singular, devotional. (Risk: nears spiritual-theater; restraint keeps
glow honest.)

**8. The Desk.** Wheel one object among a few on a well-ordered surface; plate a
nameplate; notes a pad at the writing edge; metadata in a small reference block.
*Character:* practical, lived-in, calm.

**9. The Gallery Wall.** Wheel the central hung work with reference cards as framed
companions at a respectful remove; plate a wall label; notes a docent's card;
metadata on the back-of-frame, available on inspection. *Character:* curated,
spacious, editorial.

**10. The Almanac.** Wheel paired with dense seasonal reference; plate a header;
notes as annotations; metadata woven into reference as data of record.
*Character:* archival, trustworthy, reference-first.

## TASK 2 — Ten Relocated page concepts

Focus: profile authority / location subject / wheel dominance / city-intelligence
relationship.

**1. The Guest Room.** Location is the page's topic (headline); profile authority
sits warm and close to the wheel as the lens; wheel central but a guest-temperature
step from Profile; city intelligence a folded brochure on the side table.
*Character:* provisional, hospitable.

**2. The Field Station.** Location subject as a sited place; profile a persistent
instrument-owner badge by the wheel; wheel dominant; city intelligence a readout
of conditions. *Character:* expeditionary, evaluative.

**3. The Travel Plate.** A strong location-subject plate atop, profile lens as its
subtitle ("for {profile}"); wheel just beneath, central; city intel disclosed
below. *Character:* postcard-meets-instrument.

**4. The Overlay.** Relocated wheel foregrounded with the natal as a remembered
ghost of reference; profile authority explicit; city intel deferred. *Character:*
comparative, "who I'd become here."

**5. The Twin.** Profile and relocated charts as near-siblings, profile slightly
senior; location named as subject; city intel a quiet third. *Character:* familial,
balanced.

**6. The Dossier.** Location subject as a researched case; profile lens stamped on
it; wheel central but embedded in evidence; city intel prominent as the case file.
*Character:* investigative.

**7. The Threshold.** Location as a door being considered; profile the one standing
at it; wheel central and inviting; city intel as "what's on the other side."
*Character:* anticipatory.

**8. The Quiet Relocation.** Almost identical to Profile in bones, only cooler/
lighter; location named without heavy badge (per Badge doctrine's lean); wheel
central; city intel minimal until asked. *Character:* understated, same-organism.

**9. The Atlas Leaf.** Location as a page in an atlas; profile a reader's bookmark;
wheel as the plate's centerpiece; city intel as surrounding gazetteer facts.
*Character:* cartographic, editorial.

**10. The Audition.** Location subject "trying out" for the user; profile the one
judging; wheel central and evaluative; city intel as the place's supporting
testimony. *Character:* evaluative, open-question.

## TASK 3 — Ten Comparison page concepts

Focus: density / readability / fatigue / notes coexistence / A2A coexistence.

**1. The Long Table.** Even gridded matrix, generous row rhythm; low density per
screen, scroll for more; notes a side ledger; A2A available per-city on demand.
*Character:* fair, unhurried. *Tension:* width vs depth.

**2. The Trading Floor (cautionary).** Maximal density, many cities at once; high
readability risk, high fatigue; notes squeezed; A2A crammed. Explored as a
*boundary* of what doctrine forbids. *Character:* terminal-like — a line not to
cross.

**3. The Column Garden.** Each city a calm column with breathing room; moderate
density; notes beneath each column; A2A as an openable drawer per column.
*Character:* orderly, restful.

**4. The Spotlight Pair.** Two cities compared deeply at a time, others parked;
low simultaneous density, high readability; notes rich; A2A fully present for the
pair. *Character:* focused, low-fatigue. *Tradeoff:* fewer-at-once.

**5. The Index Matrix.** Dense but heavily structured by label column and section
collapse; readability via alignment; fatigue managed by collapsing sections; notes
per section; A2A its own collapsible block. *Character:* reference-grade.

**6. The Scorecard (cautionary).** Rows that invite ranking/verdict; collides with
the honesty invariant; explored only to mark the danger of evaluative density.
*Character:* persuasive — forbidden.

**7. The Reading Comparison.** Comparison rendered more editorially, prose-adjacent
summaries beside the matrix; lower density, higher narrative; notes integral; A2A
referenced rather than shown. *Character:* essayistic.

**8. The Layered Deck.** Cities as stacked cards flipped through; only a few dense
at once; fatigue low; notes per card; A2A on the card back. *Character:* tactile,
paced.

**9. The Observatory Comparison.** Small wheels per city above a shared fact matrix;
density moderate; wheels keep the chart central even in comparison; notes below;
A2A as a synchronized row. *Character:* chart-first comparison.

**10. The Atlas Spread.** Two-page-spread feeling: places laid like an atlas with
facts as map margins; moderate density; notes as travel annotations; A2A folded
into a back section. *Character:* cartographic, calm.

## TASK 4 — Ten Notes concepts

Exploring notebook / research journal / desk / commonplace book / field notebook —
without implementing any.

**1. The Commonplace Book.** One continuous quotation-and-thought collection,
entity-tagged; reflective, literary; Profile as its bound spine.

**2. The Research Journal.** Dated, sequential entries tied to charts/cities;
investigative; reads like an ongoing inquiry.

**3. The Field Notebook.** Quick, in-the-moment captures from Map/Relocated;
terse, immediate; meant for later gathering.

**4. The Desk Blotter.** An always-present writing margin on each page; ambient,
low-ceremony; thought captured beside the work.

**5. The Margin Glosses.** Notes as marginalia attached to entities (never rows),
surfacing beside what they annotate; scholarly, contextual.

**6. The Ledger Journal.** Structured, sectioned notebook with a sense of record;
durable, accountable; the "keeping" end of the attention arc.

**7. The Letters.** Notes framed as letters to a future self about a place;
intimate, narrative; emotionally warm.

**8. The Atelier Sketchbook.** Free, unstructured surface for half-formed
impressions; exploratory; tolerant of mess.

**9. The Clearinghouse.** Profile-centered master notebook aggregating all
entity notes with filtered views; organizational; the doctrine's master-notebook
made felt.

**10. The Quiet Room.** Minimal, distraction-free long-form writing space; hushed,
Kindle-adjacent register; for the user who lives here for an hour.

## TASK 5 — Ten table atmosphere concepts (feeling only)

**1. Nearly Invisible.** Tables that almost aren't there — pure content, breath
between rows; feels weightless, calm, maybe too quiet.

**2. Editorial.** The composed feel of a well-set magazine table; authored,
considered, warm.

**3. Archival.** The feel of a ledger in a records room; durable, trustworthy,
slightly solemn.

**4. Instrument-like.** Precise readouts on a fine tool; exact, dependable,
quietly technical.

**5. Scientific.** The clean neutrality of lab data; rigorous, unembellished,
honest.

**6. Atlas-like.** The feel of a gazetteer's fact columns; spatial, reference-rich,
inviting to browse.

**7. Manuscript.** The hand-set warmth of an old printed page; humane, textured at
the threshold of perception.

**8. Observatory Log.** The feel of recorded celestial observations; patient,
sequential, reverent toward data.

**9. Field Guide.** Approachable reference for a curious reader; friendly, legible,
lightly editorial.

**10. Ledger-Hush.** Accounting precision drained of coldness; orderly and exact
yet calm enough to dwell in for an hour.

## TASK 6 — Ten metadata treatments (strengths / weaknesses)

**1. Near-Wheel Halo.** Metadata orbiting just outside the wheel. *Strength:* binds
facts to the instrument. *Weakness:* risks crowding the wheel's quiet.

**2. Beside-Wheel Column.** A side plate of facts. *Strength:* present without
intruding; classic. *Weakness:* competes for horizontal room; weak on narrow space.

**3. Beneath-Wheel Footing.** Metadata as a base under the chart. *Strength:* keeps
wheel central, facts grounded. *Weakness:* can feel like fine print; lower
attention.

**4. Split Identity/Context.** Birth triad held in one place, technical/system in
another. *Strength:* honors the Badge doctrine's epistemic grouping. *Weakness:*
two homes to learn; risk of feeling scattered.

**5. Plate-Integrated.** Metadata absorbed into the plate. *Strength:* one tidy
identity object. *Weakness:* plate can bloat; forbidden-field creep.

**6. Disclosed Drawer.** Metadata hidden until invited. *Strength:* maximal calm;
honors disclosure instinct. *Weakness:* hides facts some users want always-on;
provenance friction.

**7. Colophon Footer.** System facts as a page colophon. *Strength:* dignifies
provenance, keeps it last. *Weakness:* easy to miss; only suits low-priority data.

**8. Instrument Readout.** Metadata styled as a quiet gauge cluster. *Strength:*
reinforces instrument character. *Weakness:* can tip toward cockpit/SaaS if loud.

**9. Inline Caption.** Facts as a caption line under the plate. *Strength:* compact,
readable. *Weakness:* limited room; long names strain it.

**10. Layered Tiers.** Tier-1 identity always visible, tiers 2–4 progressively
quieter/optional. *Strength:* directly encodes importance hierarchy. *Weakness:*
needs careful restraint to avoid feeling engineered.

## TASK 7 — Wheel dominance explored

**Wheel dominates heavily.** *Gained:* unmistakable center, maximal wonder, the
chart as undisputed subject; the product feels singular and reverent. *Lost:*
room for the cognitive work (tables, comparison, notes); risk the instrument
becomes a *picture* to admire rather than a tool to think with; long working
sessions may feel starved of working surface.

**Wheel dominates moderately.** *Gained:* the wheel stays clearly central and
authoritative while leaving genuine room for tables, metadata, and notes to do
real work; best balance of wonder and analysis; sustains a 60-minute session.
*Lost:* a touch of the heavy version's awe; requires constant discipline to keep
the wheel from being nibbled by surrounding elements.

**Wheel shares authority.** *Gained:* maximal room for dense analysis and
comparison; powerful for working-heavy surfaces; tables breathe. *Lost:* the
genetic center weakens; the product risks feeling like a data tool with a chart
attached rather than a chart instrument; family resemblance (centering instinct)
frays if pushed too far.

Reading across the three: the doctrines lean toward **moderate dominance** as the
sustainable center of gravity, with *heavy* reserved for moments of encounter
(enlarged/dedicated wheel views) and *shared* tolerated only on the most
analysis-dominant surfaces (Comparison) — but this board only maps the
tradeoffs, it does not choose.

## TASK 8 — Family resemblance across the concepts

**Most connected (carry the organism well):** The Observatory, The Hearth, The
Reading Room, The Gallery Wall, The Desk (Profile); The Guest Room, The Quiet
Relocation, The Twin (Relocated); The Long Table, The Column Garden, The
Observatory Comparison (Comparison); The Clearinghouse, The Margin Glosses, The
Quiet Room (Notes); Editorial, Instrument-like, Ledger-Hush, Field Guide (tables).

*Why:* they all keep the wheel central, identity close and interpretation outward,
pacing unhurried, and atmosphere as quiet vitality. They differ as *dialects* —
register and temperature — while sharing the bones. A user would walk among them
and never feel they left the house.

**Most fragmented (strain the organism):** The Cockpit and The Shrine (Profile),
The Trading Floor and The Scorecard (Comparison) — and any metadata treatment that
crowds the wheel (Near-Wheel Halo pushed too far) or any table atmosphere taken to
an extreme (Nearly Invisible into deadness; Scientific into coldness).

*Why:* they break a load-bearing trait — Cockpit risks control-clutter, Shrine
risks spiritual-theater, Trading Floor risks data-terminal density, Scorecard
breaks the honesty invariant. Each is *coherent alone* but would feel like a
different product beside the others; the seam between rooms would become visible.

The lesson the board surfaces: resemblance survives differences of *character and
mood* but dies at violations of the six load-bearing traits (wheel centrality,
centering instinct, identity-close/interpretation-outward, pacing, attentive-not-
performing temperament, honesty).

## TASK 9 — The five most promising directions (candidates, not answers)

1. **Profile as a Hearth/Reading-Room blend** — warm, identity-forward, single
   contemplative column with the wheel composed at its heart; strongest carrier of
   "home within the home."
2. **Relocated as The Quiet Relocation** — Profile's bones held a degree cooler,
   location named without heavy badge; maximal family resemblance, honors the
   subject/authority doctrine cleanly.
3. **Comparison as The Column Garden / Observatory Comparison** — calm columns with
   moderate density and optional small wheels, A2A and notes on invited disclosure;
   discernment without exhaustion.
4. **Notes as The Clearinghouse with a Quiet-Room register** — entity-attached,
   Profile-aggregated, but writing itself in a hushed long-form space; satisfies
   both the ignorer and the hours-long dweller.
5. **Tables in an Editorial / Ledger-Hush atmosphere** — alive and humane without
   decoration; the "instrument with an editorial hand" made felt, with moderate
   wheel dominance throughout.

These five cohere with each other and with all ten doctrines — but they are the
*strongest candidates to test*, not selections.

## TASK 10 — Things we cannot know until we see them

Questions that have quietly migrated from *philosophical* to *visual* — answerable
only by looking, not reasoning:

- **How much wheel dominance actually feels right** — "moderate" is a word until we
  see a real wheel against real tables; only the eye can locate the line between
  enthroned and starved.
- **Whether moderate density reads as calm or as sparse** — the exact point where a
  comparison table becomes either restful or under-informative is a perceptual fact,
  not a principle.
- **Whether the home/guest temperature difference registers** — "one degree cooler"
  must be *seen* to know if it reads as kinship, as nothing, or as diminishment.
- **Where metadata stops grounding and starts crowding** — proximity to the wheel
  has a felt threshold no doctrine can pre-set.
- **Whether long names actually break rhythm or are absorbed** — only real strings
  in a real measure reveal it.
- **Whether tables feel "alive" without atmosphere, or need the first whisper of
  it** — the QA finding (no row hover, no row state) can only be judged once seen in
  motion.
- **Whether the six rooms truly feel like one house** — family resemblance is, in
  the end, a *perception*; it can be designed for but only confirmed by walking
  through.
- **Whether "editorial" and "instrument-like" can coexist in one table** — they
  reconcile in words easily; the eye decides if they reconcile in fact.
- **Whether disclosure feels like relief or like hiding** — the emotional valence of
  collapsed detail is only knowable in interaction.
- **Where the seam between dialects becomes a border** — the fragmentation line from
  Task 8 is ultimately a thing you *notice* or don't, not a thing you derive.

These are the questions that should now drive *seeing* rather than more writing.
The doctrine phase has done its work; the landscape above shows where the open
questions have become visual. The next movement is to look.

Exploration only. No mockups, layouts, implementation plans, or chosen winners.
