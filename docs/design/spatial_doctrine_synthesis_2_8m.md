# Spatial Doctrine Synthesis — Phase 2.8M

Status: philosophy / synthesis
Type: orientation document — the *work* space performs, not where things go
Constraint: no archaeology, no inventory, no implementation, no CSS, no responsive
rules, no mockups, no wireframes, no component specs, no placements. Philosophy only.

Question answered: not "where should components go?" but **"what work should space
perform?"**

Synthesized against established doctrine: the product is a relocation
**instrument**; the wheel is the visual center (wonder) and the tables the
cognitive center (clarity); color *orients but never judges*; typography *states
importance truthfully*; atmosphere is *quiet vitality*. Space is the medium in
which all of these are arranged into a place a mind can inhabit. **Space is not the
absence of content; it is content's context — a meaning-making layer.**

---

## 0. Premise

Every other doctrine decides the *character* of things; space decides their
*relationships* — what is near what, what is given room, what is held apart, what
is allowed to be still. A user does not read space, yet space governs how
everything else is read: it sets the pace of attention, assigns importance through
proximity and prominence, and decides whether a surface feels like a place to
dwell or a panel to process. In an instrument meant to be inhabited for an hour at
a time, **space is the difference between a room and a form.** Its governing
instinct is the same as the rest of the product's: restraint in service of a
person thinking.

---

## PROMPT 1 — What empty space is doing

Empty space in this product is doing real work, not waiting to be filled. Ranked
by importance:

**Primary:**
- **Contemplation** — the most important. Space is what gives the user permission
  to *dwell* rather than *complete*. A surface that breathes says "sit with this";
  a surface that crowds says "get through this." For a life decision, room to think
  is the product's deepest courtesy.
- **Orientation** — space tells the user, pre-verbally, what belongs together and
  what is separate, where the center is and where the margins are. Proximity is
  meaning; the eye learns the structure of a page from its spacing before it reads
  a word.
- **Authority** — space confers importance. What is given room reads as
  significant; what is surrounded by stillness commands attention. Space is how the
  product says "this matters" without raising its voice.

**Secondary (real but in service of the above):**
- **Breathing room** — the baseline legibility and calm that prevents fatigue; the
  precondition for contemplation rather than its substance.
- **Separation** — keeping distinct things distinct; necessary hygiene, but the
  lightest job, and the one most easily over-served with lines when space alone
  would do.
- **Anticipation** — space can hint that there is more to find, rewarding the
  curiosity the Map awakens; valuable but occasional, never a tease.

The principle: **space exists first to let a person think, second to help them
understand structure, and third to assign importance** — and only after those, to
separate and to invite. Emptiness is not waste; it is where attention rests and
meaning forms.

## PROMPT 2 — Space's responsibilities toward the wheel

The wheel is the central instrument, beheld for ten seconds or worked around for an
hour. Surrounding space is its custodian.

**Space's responsibilities toward the wheel:**
- **Enthrone it** — give the wheel enough room that it reads, instantly and
  always, as the center of its surface. The wheel's authority is expressed largely
  through the space around it; a wheel with room is a wheel in command.
- **Protect its stillness** — keep the immediate surround of the wheel calm, so the
  eye can rest on it in contemplation without competing pulls. The wheel needs a
  zone of quiet, like a held breath.
- **Hold its centrality through long work** — even when the user is deep in
  surrounding tables for an hour, space must keep the wheel present and central,
  the thing all the work refers back to. The wheel anchors; space keeps the anchor
  in place.

**What space must never do to the wheel:**
- **Crowd it** — never let plates, metadata, controls, or notes press in close
  enough to diminish it. Proximity steals authority; anything jammed against the
  wheel competes with it.
- **Displace it** — never push the wheel out of its central, immediately-present
  position in service of something else; the wheel earns the room, and other
  things arrange around it, not the reverse.
- **Strand it** — the opposite failure: never give the wheel so much empty
  isolation that it floats, disconnected from the work that depends on it. The
  wheel must be central *and* connected — enthroned, not exiled.

The principle: **space serves the wheel by giving it room and quiet without ever
isolating it from the thinking it anchors.**

## PROMPT 3 — How spatial hierarchy mediates the unresolved tensions

These tensions (profile authority vs location subject; wheel vs plate / notes /
tables / controls) are conflicts of *importance*, and space is how importance is
adjudicated without a single thing having to shout.

The mediating idea is a **gravitational hierarchy**: the wheel is the center of
gravity, and everything else finds its distance from that center according to how
much it serves the act of reading the chart. Space resolves each tension by
*proximity and room*, not by suppression:

- **Profile authority vs location subject** — the location may be the page's
  *subject* (what it is about), but the profile is its *authority* (the lens). Space
  resolves this by letting the subject occupy the page's *topic position* while the
  authority remains quietly *present and proximate to the wheel* — the lens is never
  far from the chart it governs. Subject gets the headline; authority keeps its
  seat beside the instrument.
- **Wheel vs plate** — the plate orients and then yields. Space gives the plate
  enough room to be read as identity, then *defers* — the plate frames, the wheel
  commands. The plate is a title above a work, not a rival to it.
- **Wheel vs tables** — the tables are cognitively dominant but spatially
  subordinate. Space lets them be expansive *in their own region* while keeping that
  region clearly *downstream* of the wheel — the eye descends from chart to ledger.
- **Wheel vs notes** — notes are a supporting layer; space keeps them available but
  *peripheral to the chart's center*, never pressing into the wheel's quiet.
- **Wheel vs controls** — controls frame the work; space keeps them *present at the
  edges of attention*, reachable without ever competing with the wheel for the
  center.

The principle: **spatial hierarchy mediates by distance from the wheel** — the more
something is the chart itself, the closer and more central; the more it is *about*
or *around* the chart, the more it arranges at a respectful remove. No tension is
resolved by removal; each is resolved by *where it is allowed to stand* relative to
the center of gravity.

## PROMPT 4 — Spatial behavior across modes of thinking

Each page has a dominant activity — discovery, orientation, discernment,
evaluation, research, reflection — and space should *change its behavior* to suit
the kind of thinking, while keeping one spatial *temperament* so the product stays
one organism.

- **Map / discovery** — space is most *expansive and open*; room to roam, the world
  given breadth, little enclosure. Thinking here is wandering, and space should feel
  like sky.
- **Profile / orientation** — space is *centered and composed*; everything gathers
  around the wheel, calm and intimate. Thinking is settling-into-yourself, and space
  should feel like a still center.
- **Comparison / discernment** — space is *ordered and even*; regular, gridded
  calm that lets difference become legible without favor. Thinking is weighing, and
  space should feel level and fair.
- **Relocated / evaluation** — space echoes Profile but holds the chart slightly
  more *provisionally*; room to imagine without committing. Thinking is trying-on,
  and space should feel like an open question.
- **City Intelligence / research** — space is *sectioned and navigable*; grounded,
  legible territory the eye can move through deliberately. Thinking is studying, and
  space should feel like a well-organized field guide.
- **Notes / reflection** — space is most *intimate and uncluttered*; a quiet margin
  around the user's own thought. Thinking is reflection, and space should feel like
  a private desk.

What keeps these one organism is a **constant spatial temperament** — the same
generosity, the same restraint, the same refusal to crowd — modulated in *degree*,
never in *kind*. The product breathes more openly toward discovery and more
intimately toward reflection, but it is always the same breath. The principle:
**space adapts its proportions to the mode of thinking while keeping its manners
constant.**

## PROMPT 5 — Principles governing spatial tradeoffs

Long names, large wheels, dense tables, and extensive notes all compete for finite
space. The contest is real and cannot be wished away; what governs it is a set of
*priorities*, not a layout.

Governing principles:
- **The wheel's centrality is close to inviolable.** When something must yield, the
  wheel yields last; its room is the product's spine. Most tradeoffs resolve by
  protecting the center first.
- **Importance earns room; reference earns sufficiency.** What the user is actively
  *thinking with* deserves generous space; what they merely *consult* deserves only
  enough to be legible. Space is allocated by the depth of attention a thing
  receives, not by how much it would like to occupy.
- **Density is permitted where it serves analysis, never where it serves vanity.**
  A dense table that supports discernment has earned its compression; density added
  to look substantial has not. Crowding is acceptable only as the price of genuine
  analytical value.
- **Identity is honored even when it is inconvenient.** A long name is an identity,
  not an overflow; space should flex to accommodate fullness with composure rather
  than treat the inconvenient as the exceptional. (No truncation rule is proposed;
  the point is the posture, not the mechanism.)
- **Stillness is a budget line, not leftover.** Empty space is allocated *on
  purpose*, defended like any other element; tradeoffs must not treat contemplative
  room as the first thing to sacrifice when pressure rises.

The deep principle: **space is allocated by attention and dignity, not by demand.**
The element that wants the most room is rarely the one that should get it; the
question is always *what is the user thinking with, and what deserves to be honored
whole.*

## PROMPT 6 — How space distinguishes identity from meaning

The product holds two categories: **identity** (who, where, when — fixed fact) and
**meaning** (what it suggests — interpretation). Space should keep them
*distinguishable by distance from the wheel*.

**Identity stays near the wheel.** Identity is what the chart *is* — the
irreducible who/where/when from which everything is computed. It belongs close to
the instrument because it is the instrument's ground truth; the user should feel
identity and chart as a single, trustworthy core. Identity near the center also
reflects its certainty: these are facts, and facts sit at the stable heart of the
page.

**Interpretation drifts outward.** Meaning is *derived*, more tentative, more
plural — and space should express that by letting it occupy the regions *around and
beyond* the identity core. Interpretation is where the user does the open, uncertain
work of thinking, and that work wants room further from the center, where there is
space to consider, weigh, and dissent.

*Why this matters:* the spatial separation encodes an *epistemic* one. Keeping fact
close and certain, and interpretation outward and roomy, quietly teaches the user
what to trust as given and what to hold as suggestion — without a word. It also
protects integrity: identity near the wheel cannot be confused with the
product's *opinions*, which live at a deliberate remove. The principle: **space
distinguishes identity from meaning as core from periphery — certainty held close,
interpretation given room to breathe** — so the user always feels the difference
between what *is* and what something might *mean*.

## PROMPT 7 — Supporting transitions between wonder, discernment, reflection

The Map holds wonder, the tables discernment, the notebook reflection — three
states of mind that ask for three qualities of space, joined by smooth transitions
rather than hard cuts.

- **Wonder should feel expansive.** Space opens, horizons widen, enclosure
  loosens; the user feels possibility and breadth. Wonder needs *air*.
- **Discernment should feel concentrated.** Space tightens into order and focus;
  the world narrows to the comparison at hand, even and exacting. Discernment needs
  *containment* — not crowding, but a gathered, level field where judgment can work.
- **Reflection should feel intimate.** Space draws in close and quiet, a small
  uncluttered margin around the user's own thought. Reflection needs *hush*.

The transitions between these are themselves a spatial responsibility: moving from
the Map's openness to a table's concentration should feel like *focusing*, not like
a wall slamming down; moving from analysis to notes should feel like the room
*quieting*, not like changing buildings. Space supports the transitions by changing
*gradually in proportion and density* so the user's state of mind is carried, not
jolted — expansive easing into concentrated easing into intimate. The principle:
**space gives each state its native proportion — air for wonder, containment for
discernment, hush for reflection — and moves between them as a gradient of breath,
never a series of doors.**

## PROMPT 8 — The role of context when attention narrows

When a wheel enlarges into a modal or dedicated view, attention deliberately
narrows to a single thing — and the question is what happens to everything else.

Context should **become memory and reference, not vanish.** The surrounding work
should recede — quieted, set back, released from active competition — so the
enlarged wheel can have the user's whole attention. But it should not be
*destroyed*: the user chose to look closer at something that exists *within* a
context, and severing that context entirely would turn a deepening into a
displacement, leaving them unsure how to get back to where they were thinking.

So when attention narrows, surrounding context should:
- **Recede, not disappear** — step back into stillness, present as the felt frame
  of where the user still is, even if not actively legible.
- **Become memory** — the user should retain the sense of the room they came from,
  so the enlarged view feels like *leaning in* rather than *leaving*.
- **Remain reference** — available to return to without friction, so narrowing
  attention is a reversible act of focus, not a commitment.

The deeper principle: **narrowing attention should feel like focusing within a
place, not teleporting to another.** Enlargement is the user pulling one thing
closer, and the surrounding space should behave the way a real room does when you
lean toward one object — the rest softens into periphery but is still *there*, still
home. Context, when attention narrows, becomes the quiet assurance that the user has
not lost their place.

## PROMPT 9 — Spatial mistakes that betray the genre

Each failure is a misuse of space's purpose.

- **SaaS** — space treated as *real estate to be utilized*; every region filled,
  efficiency maximized, emptiness read as waste. The page becomes a control surface,
  not a thinking surface.
- **Dashboard thinking** — space carved into *equal panels of simultaneous
  information*; everything visible at once, nothing given primacy. Hierarchy
  dissolves; the wheel loses its center; the user is shown everything and helped
  with nothing.
- **Control clutter** — space surrendered to *affordances*; buttons and options
  crowding the work until the instrument is buried in its own controls. Action
  smothers contemplation.
- **Feature creep** — space conceded incrementally to *new things*, each justified
  alone, together strangling the room to think. Stillness is eroded one addition at
  a time.
- **Comparison exhaustion** — space *compressed past analysis into endurance*;
  density pursued until reading becomes labor and discernment becomes fatigue.
- **Notebook fragmentation** — space *broken into disconnected pieces*; reflection
  scattered across compartments until the user's continuous thought feels chopped,
  and the notebook stops feeling like one quiet place.

The common misunderstanding behind all of them: **treating space as emptiness to be
eliminated rather than as the medium that makes thinking possible.** Each failure
sees room as waste, density as value, and presence as productivity — and so spends
the very stillness, hierarchy, and quiet that let a person think. The shared error
is *horror vacui*: the inability to leave space alone, to trust that emptiness is
doing work.

## PROMPT 10 — How inhabiting the product's space should have felt

After three hours researching cities, ninety minutes comparing charts, and
forty-five minutes writing notes, the user closes the laptop — and the lasting
feeling should be of having spent that time in *a calm, well-proportioned place that
never once crowded them.* Not a tool they operated, but a room they inhabited: one
where there was always enough space to think, where the important thing was always
plainly at the center and the rest waited patiently at the edges until called,
where dense work was demanding but never suffocating and quiet work was private and
unhurried. They should feel *rested rather than wrung out* — surprised, perhaps,
that so much was done in a space that never felt busy. They should remember the
*clarity* of the hours, not the *effort*, and carry a quiet sense that the product
made room for them — held their attention gently, honored what mattered, and left
them the stillness to reach their own conclusions. The deepest success is that the
space should have felt *generous* — that the product, even while holding wheels and
tables and names and notes all competing for finite room, somehow always seemed to
have enough air to breathe.

Philosophy only. No layouts, wireframes, responsive rules, component specs, or
placements. The arrangement of the product, when it comes, must answer to this.
