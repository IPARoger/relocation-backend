## Professional Map Workflow

Professionals should be able to:

1. Select desired placements or variables.
2. Search the map for regions where desired variables overlap.
3. Add Exclude / NOT variables for placements they explicitly want to avoid.
4. View excluded regions in a quieter warning language, likely greyscale or muted overlay.
5. Use solo and mute controls to isolate or declutter layers.
6. Explore multiple candidate regions quickly.
7. Click cities or locations to inspect a popup planetary house breakdown.
8. Open a full relocated chart for deeper inspection.
9. Review aspects, including aspects to angles.
10. Save promising charts to favorites.
11. Compare charts and tables side by side.
12. Use AI / Astro Assist only as an assistant, not as a replacement for professional judgment.

---

## Desired Placement Search

Professionals may search for placements such as:

- Saturn in the 10th house
- Sagittarius rising
- Moon conjunct IC
- Sun in the 1st house
- Venus in the 7th house
- Jupiter in the 8th house
- Multiple simultaneous variables

The map should make it easy to see where desired conditions overlap.

The system should support exploration, not force a single "best" answer.

---

## Exclude / NOT Variables

Professionals may also select placements they explicitly do not want.

Examples:

- Moon in the 8th house
- Saturn in the 1st house
- Saturn in the 12th house
- Mars in the 4th house
- Neptune in the 6th house

Excluded variables should be visually distinct from positive search variables.

Potential language:
"Excluded variables show regions that match conditions you asked to avoid. These are not moral judgments or universal bad placements. They are simply places that conflict with your current search strategy."

---

## Solo And Mute Controls

The map should support:
- muting individual layers temporarily,
- soloing one layer for inspection,
- restoring all layers,
- and decluttering dense multi-variable searches.

Potential language:
"Use Solo to inspect one condition at a time. Use Mute to temporarily hide a layer without removing it from your investigation."

## Inspection Workflow

Recommended professional workflow:

1. Search for desired placements.
2. Add exclusions for known dealbreakers.
3. Inspect overlap regions.
4. Click candidate cities.
5. Review the popup breakdown.
6. Open the full relocated chart.
7. Compare aspects and angle aspects.
8. Save favorites.
9. Compare finalists side by side.

Potential language:
"The map is designed for broad exploration. The popup gives a first-pass breakdown. The full relocated chart provides the deeper inspection layer."

## Helper Layers

The product may eventually include optional helper layers that highlight broadly recognized traditional or practical placement categories.

Examples:

- traditional dignity-based strengths,
- traditional weakness/difficulty categories,
- broadly recognized friendly placements,
- broadly recognized difficult placements not formally covered by essential dignity language.

Important:
These helper layers must remain optional and explanatory.

They must not imply that charts are inherently good or bad independent of user intention.

Potential language:
"Helper layers are optional reference tools. They highlight placements that many astrologers commonly consider strong, difficult, friendly, or challenging. Your relocation intention remains more important than any generic strength category."

---

## Intention Remains Primary

A chart is not "better" in the abstract.

A chart is better or worse relative to:
- the user's intention,
- life phase,
- tolerance for tradeoffs,
- practical constraints,
- and relationship to the archetype.

Potential language:
"A good chart for the wrong intention may still be the wrong relocation. Always evaluate placements in relation to the purpose of the move."

## Astro Assist Substitution Guidance

Astro Assist may eventually suggest alternatives when exact selected variables are unavailable, impractical, or paired with unacceptable downsides.

Example:
If Sun in the 1st house is only available over the Pacific Ocean or remote wilderness, Astro Assist might search for a major city where the Sun trines or sextiles the Ascendant within a tight orb.

Potential language:
"Exact placements are not always available in practical locations. Astro Assist can help search for symbolic substitutes, such as angle aspects or nearby configurations that approximate the intention of your original search."

Important:
The professional remains in charge.

Astro Assist suggests.
It does not decide.

## Additive And Subtractive Relocation

Professional use should support both:

Subtractive relocation:
- moving away from difficult placements,
- reducing chronic friction,
- removing unwanted house emphasis.

Additive relocation:
- adding desired placements,
- increasing supportive angularity,
- strengthening intentional themes.

Potential language:
"Relocation strategy often begins by reducing chronic difficulty, then searches for places that also add desired strengths."

## Layer 1 Work-Order Priority Explanation

Layer 1 runtime may use deterministic work-order priorities for computational economy and product responsiveness.

Examples:
- user-selected foreground search first,
- current requested variables before background cache,
- boundary refinement before broad speculative cache warming,
- user intent before zoom continuation,
- cache warming only after the foreground search is served.

Important:
These work-order priorities do NOT imply symbolic importance, astrological value, or location quality.

Potential language:
"Runtime priority controls what the system calculates first. It does not mean one placement is astrologically more important than another. User-selected variables always override generic defaults."

## 2-Degree Subsequent House Gradient — Popup Copy Draft

Purpose:
Explain the late-house / subsequent-house gradient policy in user-facing language.

Draft language:
"When a planet is very close to the next house cusp, we show a subtle transition zone rather than treating the placement as completely isolated in one house. By default, the final 2 degrees before the next house cusp may be shown as a gradient into the following house. This reflects the practical reality that late-house planets often express some relationship to the approaching house boundary."

Clarifying language:
"This is a display and interpretive-assistance convention. It does not move the planet. The exact astronomical position remains unchanged."

Professional note:
"Professionals may adjust or disable this convention depending on their house-system preferences."

## High Latitude / 65-Degree Latitude Explanation Draft

Purpose:
Explain why high-latitude regions may require caution.

Draft language:
"Some house systems become unstable, distorted, or difficult to interpret at high latitudes. Above roughly 65 degrees latitude, house cusps and angular relationships can behave in ways that require additional caution."

Potential user-facing note:
"High-latitude results are not necessarily wrong, but they may require advanced interpretation. We may limit, flag, or add warnings for high-latitude results until the system has stronger validation coverage."

Professional note:
"Advanced users may later be allowed to inspect or override latitude constraints, but default users should receive clear cautionary language."

---

## Placidus vs Other House Systems — Explanation Draft

Purpose:
Explain default house-system assumptions.

Draft language:
"The platform may initially use Placidus as the default house system because it is widely used in modern Western astrology and aligns with the project's initial validation path."

Clarifying language:
"Other house systems may produce different house placements, especially near cusps or at high latitudes. These differences do not mean one calculation is fake and another is real. They reflect different geometric frameworks."

Professional note:
"Future versions may support Equal House, Whole Sign, Koch, and Vedic/sidereal frameworks. House-system selection belongs to the geometric framework layer, not the interpretive AI layer."

## Transits — Research And Experimentation Draft

Purpose:
Explain the project's stance on transits in relocation work.

Draft language:
"The platform may eventually support transit research in relation to relocated charts, natal charts, or both. Transit methodology varies among astrologers, and the product should present these tools as research and experimentation aids rather than as settled doctrine."

Possible caution:
"Some astrologers prefer transits to natal houses; others investigate transits to relocated houses. The platform may support both approaches with clear labeling and methodological warnings."

Professional note:
"Transit modes should remain explicitly labeled so users understand which chart framework is being used."
