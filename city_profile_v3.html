Overview

Lisbon is Portugal’s capital and largest city, with established public services, international connectivity, and a sizable long-term foreign resident population.

City proper: ~505,000; metro ~2.9 million — a major city, not a village or small town.

Crime tends to be concentrated in specific districts. Most daily activity areas are considered comfortable for residents and visitors.

Portugal maintains a stable democratic system with predictable institutions.

  • Speech climate: Freedom of expression protected under EU standards.
  • Alcohol restrictions: Legal purchase age 18; widely available.
  • Drug policy: Personal possession of small amounts decriminalized.
  • Traveler restrictions: Standard EU entry norms.
  • Cultural climate: Urban Lisbon generally socially liberal.
  • Female traveler considerations: Normal urban awareness applies.
  • LGBTQ considerations: Same-sex marriage recognized.

Strong Expat Community — integrated with local culture; large long-term international population.

Metro, bus, tram, international airport (LIS), reliable utilities and mobile coverage.

Public healthcare: SNS provides subsidized care to legal residents.

Private healthcare: Private clinics offer faster access; widely used by expats.

International hospitals: Several serve international patients with English-speaking staff.

Insurance notes: Non-EU residents typically need private insurance until SNS enrollment.

Infrastructure & Logistics

Metro covers major corridors; walkability high in central areas.

Home fiber 100–1000 Mbps typical; 4G/5G coverage strong.

Lisbon Humberto Delgado (LIS), 7 km from center.

US citizens: visa-free up to 90 days Schengen; longer stays require residence permit.

Tax residency typically after 183 days or establishing habitual residence.

Visa Information

For United States Citizens

US passport holders may visit Portugal without a visa for up to 90 days within any 180-day Schengen period.

Culture

Slower pace than Northern European capitals; strong outdoor culture.

Historically Catholic; secular daily life common in urban Lisbon.

Seafood, grilled meats, olive oil, seasonal produce.

Alcohol & Drugs
  • Drinking age: 18
  • Wine and beer widely available
  • Personal cannabis possession decriminalized; distribution illegal

Neighborhood markets and tascas serve daily set menus at modest prices.

Gulbenkian gardens, Eduardo VII park, Atlantic coastline by transit.
