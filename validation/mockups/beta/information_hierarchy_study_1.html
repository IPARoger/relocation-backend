Information Hierarchy Study 1

First-principles study: how professional dense-information systems make repeated facts easy to ignore, instantly recoverable — without disabled, hidden, or unimportant registers. Nine technique families + one anti-pattern. Cities: Boston, New York (high duplicate pair), Omaha (divergent control).

Variants: AP anti-pattern · R1 weight+tracking · R2 saturation · R3 micro-wash · R4 edge · R5 grain · R6 optical size · R7 PIH composite · R8 A2A split

Ten dimensions evaluated (see report)

  1. Ink hierarchy — warm-shift, not cool-grey default
  2. Saturation hierarchy — toward paper, full opacity
  3. Figure-ground — cell-local micro-wash
  4. Local contrast — weight / size split
  5. Edge treatments — inset hairlines, brackets
  6. Texture — ground grain, not glyph fade
  7. Peripheral cues — tracking, optical size
  8. Color-temperature — paper-family recede
  9. Density-based — rejected for this UI
  10. Cell-local — required; no row fade

AP — Anti-pattern (production-like)

Opacity 0.48 + cool slate on entire cell. Rejected — disabled register, dignity tint collapse.
