# Chronological Raw Extraction

## Early State — Renderer Confusion / Aura Failure

The renderer phase began with repeated dissatisfaction around:

* blur-based gradients,
* glow-like rendering,
* atmospheric haze,
* diffuse alpha fading,
* “space nebula” aesthetics,
* spraypaint softness,
* and visible banding/striations.

Repeated user reactions:

* “This looks blurry.”
* “This looks diffuse.”
* “This looks fizzy.”
* “This looks like aura/fog.”
* “This still looks computerized.”
* “This is not materially coherent.”
* “The centerline is too fat.”
* “The centerline is disconnected from the body.”
* “It looks like a curve on top of a curve.”
* “Photoshop can do this.”

A major recurring problem:
the renderer kept psychologically reading as:

* emitted light,
* aura,
* glow,
* blur,
* fog,
* haze,
* feather,
* or airbrush softness.

Rather than:

* solid transported material.

This became one of the central doctrine pivots of the chat.

---

## Transfer-Function Exploration Phase

The process temporarily narrowed into pure transfer-function studies.

Important realization:
geometry itself was not yet the main problem.

The problem was:

* perceptual material continuity,
* ridge authority,
* tonal residency,
* mid-range compression,
* and release timing.

Several geometry-free validation boards were created:

* rectangular domains only,
* no maps,
* no curves,
* no astrology,
* no topology.

Candidate transfer functions explored:

* exponential pressure
* harmonic pressure
* logistic release
* fibonacci power
* enamel membrane
* plasma density
* gaussian comparison
* linear fade comparison
* hybrid candidates

User repeatedly emphasized:

* gradients were dropping off too quickly,
* mids disappeared too early,
* translucent regions became flat and lifeless,
* crescendos/diminuendos psychologically peaked too early,
* “forte piano” instead of “forte diminuendo piano.”

One major insight:
the approved transfer profile (#8 candidate) had:

* proper release timing,
* proper mid-body retention,
* proper psychological momentum.

This became the locked reference.

---

## Critical Pivot — “STOP THINKING ABOUT AURAS”

One of the major doctrine crystallizations:

The renderer is NOT:

* an aura,
* a glow,
* a spline blur,
* gaussian falloff,
* alpha haze,
* feathered polygon,
* or emitted light field.

The renderer IS:

* a transported material strip.

This became the single most important conceptual breakthrough.

Exact conceptual doctrine:

* the approved rectangle is a material sample,
* the renderer bends/deforms/transports this material through geometry,
* the renderer does NOT emit opacity outward from a line,
* the center ridge is embedded inside the same material,
* orthogonal slices should resemble the approved rectangle profile.

This caused a complete renderer philosophy inversion.

The correct mental model became:

> “Illustrator/Photoshop gradient material warped through space.”

NOT:

> “a glow around a curve.”

This dramatically improved renderer coherence.

---

## Rejection of Spline-Distance Rendering

A major renderer governance realization:

The renderer must NOT ask:

> “how far is this pixel from the spline?”

Instead it must ask:

> “where is this pixel inside the transported strip coordinates?”

This became:

* local s coordinate
* local u coordinate
* texture-coordinate transport

Per-pixel contract:

1. determine local station coordinate s
2. determine local cross-strip coordinate u
3. sample locked material profile at u
4. write RGB directly

Important doctrine:

* opacity is not the primary rendering mechanism
* RGB/material transport is the primary mechanism
* centerline is not a separate stroke
* ridge is embedded inside the material body

This froze the renderer architecture.

---

## Orthogonal Transport / Side-Local Scaling

After the material-strip pivot, the renderer moved into:

* orthogonal transport,
* local proportional scaling,
* asymmetry survivability,
* and independent side normalization.

Key validation:
each side independently normalizes:

* local width,
* ridge compression,
* body width,
* edge timing.

Important distinction:
a 3° side is not:

* a cropped 10° side.

It is:

* the same full material hierarchy compressed proportionally.

This became one of the strongest structural validations of the renderer.

---

## Texture-Only Refinement Phase

Once architecture stabilized, refinements became:

* texture-only,
* not transport redesign.

This distinction became extremely important.

Repeated refinements:

* narrower ridge
* stronger ridge authority
* compressed adjacent dark body
* faster early release
* less pale-edge residency
* restrained outer mids
* reduced lingering white territory

Important renderer governance:

* no blur
* no glow
* no feather
* no post-smoothing
* no gaussian haze
* no spline-distance fading
* no separate centerline stroke

The renderer was now considered:

* structurally stable,
* aesthetically provisional.

## Surface Coherence / Material Language

Another major insight:
the problem was not only gradient timing.

It was:

* surface coherence.

The renderer still sometimes looked:

* stratified,
* pixelated,
* mechanically layered,
* speckled,
* “computerized.”

Discussion evolved toward:

* matte surfaces,
* enamel continuity,
* coherent material feel,
* subtle gloss,
* non-plastic premium finish,
* anti-“Batman latex” fake luxury.

Important doctrine:
future design AI may refine:

* material language,
* texture vocabulary,
* palette systems,
* premium coherence,
  without reopening renderer architecture.

This created the:

> frozen core / flexible surface
> doctrine.

* subtle,

* overlap hot-zone doctrine.

None frozen yet.
